SEO Isn't Dead: What Still Matters in the Age of AI Search

Search is changing, but people and search engines still need to find, understand and trust the content on your website.

SEO still helps search engines understand your site

Every so often, a change in search brings the same question: is SEO dead? It comes up again as AI-generated answers become more visible and people find information in different ways.

I don't think SEO has disappeared. The way people discover pages may be changing, but a business still needs a website that can be found and understood, with content that gives people a reason to pay attention.

Before a page can appear in search, search engines need to be able to access it and make sense of what it is about. Clear page titles, useful headings, descriptive links and a sensible site structure all help people find their way around, too.

That matters whether someone arrives through a traditional search result, an AI-generated answer or a link from another page. Google's guidance on AI features in Search says the same SEO best practices remain relevant.

Useful content still needs a point

Having a page about a subject is not the same as giving someone a useful reason to read it. The content needs to answer the question they came with, explain what matters and help them work out what to do next.

That takes more than including the right phrases. The ideas need to fit together, and the article needs a clear takeaway. I wrote more about that in Why Human Writers Still Matter in Search .

Links connect the wider picture

Links help people move between related pages and help search engines discover how those pages fit together. A useful internal link gives someone a natural next step when they want to explore a subject further.

For example, being mentioned in an AI answer might help someone notice a business, but it doesn't tell us whether they will visit. I explored that difference in Being Cited by AI Search Is Not the Same as Being Chosen .

Look beyond visibility on its own

Rankings, traffic and citations can show that a page is being seen. They do not tell the whole story. It is also useful to look at what people do when they reach the website: whether they find the information they need, visit another relevant page or get in touch.

SEO still has a role in helping the right people find a business. The work is to make the site clear and accessible, create content that is worth someone's time, and give them a sensible route to take the next step. Search may keep changing, but those needs remain.
