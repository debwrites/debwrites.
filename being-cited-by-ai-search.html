AI Search & Visibility

Being Cited by AI Search Is Not the Same as Being Chosen

19 October 2026

Google is making links to source websites more visible in its AI-generated search results. That sounds like good news for businesses whose content is used in an answer. It could give someone a reason to visit the website behind it.

What does visibility lead to?

But I also wonder how much of that journey a business will actually be able to see. Someone might read the answer, take in the information and carry on without clicking. The content may have helped them, but the business might never know.

Good content still matters. If it answers people's questions clearly, it has a better chance of being found through Google and of helping someone understand a topic. But being seen is only one part of the picture.

Rankings and traffic can tell us whether people are finding a page. They don't tell us on their own whether the page has helped someone, or whether they have chosen to find out more.

AI search makes that harder to judge. Someone could see a business cited in an answer and remember it without ever visiting the website. That influence may not show up in the usual website figures.

So I wouldn't look at traffic on its own. I'd want to know whether people who visit can find what they need, understand the offer and decide whether they trust the business. And whether they feel ready to take the next step.

Why would someone click?

If an AI answer has already explained the basics, the linked page needs to give someone a reason to carry on.

For me, recommendations would make a difference. If something I read pointed me towards a business, I'd want to visit its website and see what it does for myself. I'd be looking for what the business offers, how it works and what value it could bring. I'd also want to feel I could trust the service or brand.

A useful article can help with that. It can give people more detail, share an example or help them understand what to consider next. It should give them something beyond the quick answer.

What happens when they reach the website?

A click is only useful if people can make sense of the page they land on. Can they see what the business does and how it works? Can they understand the value of the offer? Does the website give them a reason to trust it?

I've visited polished websites with plenty of information and still struggled to work out exactly what the business did. I had to click around and piece it together. That's the kind of work a visitor shouldn't have to do.

The next step matters too. A call to action needs to feel right for where someone is in their decision. If they're still trying to understand the offer, they may not be ready to get in touch. The website needs to help them build that understanding first.

An article might be clear, but if the rest of the website makes it hard to understand the business or find relevant information, some of that good work is lost.

What can businesses look at now?

There's no need to rewrite every article just to try to get an AI citation. I'd start with the content and pages that matter most.

Are they answering the questions people actually have? Is there a useful example or experience that helps someone understand the subject? When a person reaches the website, can they see what the business offers and why it might be right for them?

It's also worth looking beyond clicks. Are the right people getting in touch? Are the enquiries relevant? Are they turning into customers? Those answers matter alongside traffic and search visibility, even if it isn't always possible to trace exactly where someone first heard about the business.

A citation may help someone notice or learn about a business. Good content can help them understand it. The website then needs to give them enough clarity and confidence to decide what to do next.
